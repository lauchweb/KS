The practice is rooted in an interest in images, objects, materials, places, and the ways they gather meaning through time. Attention is often drawn to things that are familiar, overlooked, worn, altered, or difficult to place.

Drawing, photography, painting, pattern, found elements, documentation, and collected material provide different ways of approaching these interests. No single medium defines the practice. Each offers a particular way of looking, recording, altering, or understanding something.

Images and objects are often considered not only for what they show, but for what they carry. A photograph can hold a particular moment while also suggesting everything that came before or after it. An object can retain evidence of use, absence, attachment, or change. Small details can become ways into something larger.

Memory is present in this relationship to things. It is not treated as something fixed or complete, but as something that shifts with distance and return. Familiar images, objects, and places can appear differently when encountered again, carrying associations that may not have been visible at first.

Materiality is equally important. Surfaces, textures, marks, repetition, wear, and physical traces can influence how an idea develops. The distinction between original and reproduction, physical and digital, permanent and temporary can become part of the work rather than something to resolve.

The practice also makes space for collecting and keeping. Photographs, drawings, fragments, references, objects, and observations can accumulate over time. Some remain dormant; others return unexpectedly and become useful in a different context.

There is an interest in what happens when these materials are placed beside one another. An image can change the reading of an object; a pattern can alter the perception of a photograph; a fragment can introduce a connection that was not previously apparent. Meaning can emerge through relationships rather than from any single element.

The practice remains open-ended. Ideas can move between forms, develop slowly, or remain unresolved. There is no requirement for everything gathered to become a finished piece. Some things are kept because they continue to hold possibility.

At its centre is a sustained attention to what is present and what is disappearing — to the physical traces left by time, the instability of memory, and the possibilities that emerge through looking closely. The work allows these things to remain open, with freedom to follow an idea wherever it leads.
