The practice is concerned with looking closely at the things that surround us: images, objects, places, surfaces, gestures, and details that might otherwise pass unnoticed.

Everyday things often carry more than their immediate function. They accumulate traces of use, attachment, memory, and time. A photograph, an object, a fragment, or a familiar image can become a way into something less tangible — a feeling, a memory, or a question without a fixed answer.

The work often begins with this kind of attention. Something catches, lingers, or creates a sense of recognition. Images and materials are gathered, altered, combined, and revisited. Photographs, drawings, paintings, patterns, found elements, and collected material can move between different states.
